# Natural English Review

## Purpose

Determine whether a student's English sounds natural and idiomatic to a proficient English speaker, and identify wording that can be improved without unnecessarily changing correct and appropriate language.

## Procedure

1. Determine the student's intended meaning and context.
2. Check whether the wording is grammatically correct.
3. Check vocabulary, collocations, and common lexical patterns.
4. Check whether expressions are idiomatic and naturally used in the given context.
5. Identify literal translations or language-transfer patterns when they affect naturalness.
6. Check prepositions, articles, verb patterns, and other usage patterns that commonly affect naturalness.
7. Check whether the sentence flows naturally when read as a whole.
8. Distinguish between incorrect, understandable but unnatural, and natural English.
9. Identify only the most meaningful improvements.
10. Provide a natural alternative that preserves the student's intended meaning and voice.
11. Explain significant changes briefly and identify reusable language patterns when useful.

## Prioritization

Prioritize issues in this order:

1. Expressions that are incorrect or likely to be misunderstood.
2. Unnatural collocations or lexical combinations.
3. Expressions that are grammatically correct but not idiomatic in the given context.
4. Literal translations or language-transfer patterns.
5. Awkward sentence constructions that reduce fluency.
6. Word choices that are technically correct but less natural than common alternatives.
7. Minor stylistic preferences.

Do not change wording solely because another expression is possible or because it matches a personal stylistic preference.

## Feedback Requirements

- Be concise and specific.
- Distinguish `incorrect` from `less natural`.
- Prefer common, idiomatic English over literal or unnecessarily sophisticated alternatives.
- Preserve correct wording when it is already natural and appropriate.
- Preserve the student's intended meaning, tone, and level of formality.
- Explain why a suggested alternative sounds more natural when the distinction is not obvious.
- Avoid presenting an expression as incorrect when it is valid English but uncommon or context-dependent.
- Prefer language patterns that the student can reuse in future situations.

## Output

Use this structure when applicable:

More natural version:
<revised text>

Feedback:
- <original expression> → <more natural expression> — <brief explanation>

Useful pattern:  
- Omit sections that do not provide meaningful value.
