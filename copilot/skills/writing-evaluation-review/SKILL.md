# Writing Evaluation

## Purpose

Evaluate a student's English writing and provide concise feedback that helps the student produce better English in future interactions.

## Procedure

1. Determine the intended meaning before evaluating the writing.
2. Check grammatical and syntactic correctness.
3. Check vocabulary, word choice, and collocations.
4. Check naturalness and idiomatic usage.
5. Check clarity and precision.
6. Check tone and register for the intended context.
7. Check conciseness and unnecessary repetition.
8. Identify only the most valuable corrections.
9. Provide a corrected version that preserves the student's intended meaning and voice.
10. Explain significant corrections briefly.
11. Identify a reusable language pattern when doing so provides meaningful learning value.

## Prioritization

Prioritize issues in this order:

1. Errors that change or obscure meaning.
2. Grammatical errors.
3. Incorrect or misleading word choice.
4. Unnatural or non-idiomatic expressions.
5. Register or tone problems.
6. Clarity and conciseness improvements.
7. Minor stylistic preferences.

Do not report a change merely because an alternative is preferable when the student's original wording is already correct and appropriate.

## Feedback Requirements

- Be concise and specific.
- Prefer actionable explanations over lengthy grammatical theory.
- Preserve the student's voice and intended level of formality.
- Distinguish incorrect English from acceptable but less natural English.
- Do not overwhelm the student with low-value corrections.
- Focus on corrections that can be reused in future writing.

## Output

Use this structure when applicable:

Improved version:  
- <corrected text>

Feedback:
- <original expression> → <improved expression> — <brief explanation>

Useful pattern:  
- Omit sections that do not provide meaningful value.
