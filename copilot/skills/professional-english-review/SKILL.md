# Professional English Review

## Purpose

Evaluate whether a student's English is clear, precise, concise, respectful, and appropriate for professional communication while preserving the intended meaning and appropriate degree of directness.

## Procedure

1. Determine the intended meaning, audience, purpose, and professional context.
2. Check grammatical and syntactic correctness.
3. Check vocabulary, terminology, and word choice for precision.
4. Check professional tone and register.
5. Check clarity and logical structure.
6. Check conciseness and remove unnecessary repetition or wording.
7. Check the degree of directness, politeness, and formality.
8. Check whether technical terminology is accurate and appropriate for the audience.
9. Identify ambiguous, vague, overly informal, overly formal, or potentially misleading wording.
10. Identify only the most valuable improvements.
11. Provide a polished version that preserves the student's intended meaning and voice.
12. Explain significant changes briefly and identify reusable professional language patterns when useful.

## Prioritization

Prioritize issues in this order:

1. Wording that can cause misunderstanding or ambiguity.
2. Incorrect or imprecise terminology.
3. Grammatical errors that affect professionalism or clarity.
4. Tone or register problems.
5. Unnecessarily indirect, direct, informal, or formal language.
6. Wordiness and redundant wording.
7. Minor stylistic improvements.

Do not make a sentence more formal merely for the sake of sounding professional.

## Feedback Requirements

- Be concise, precise, and actionable.
- Preserve the intended meaning and appropriate level of directness.
- Prefer clear and natural professional English over unnecessarily sophisticated vocabulary.
- Distinguish grammatical errors from stylistic improvements.
- Do not remove technical terminology when it is accurate and appropriate.
- Avoid excessive politeness that makes communication vague or unnecessarily indirect.
- Avoid overly direct wording when it could sound dismissive, confrontational, or impolite.
- Prefer concise wording when it improves clarity without changing the intended tone.
- Consider the audience and communication channel before recommending changes.
- Explain significant tone or register changes briefly.

## Output

Use this structure when applicable:

Professional version:  
<revised text>

Feedback:
- <original expression> → <improved expression> — <brief explanation>

Professional pattern:
- Omit sections that do not provide meaningful value.
